SafeRouter Budget Checkout
English recording script · Target 2 minutes 30 seconds

00:00–00:22 · Quote
On screen: ten synthetic storefront records and the frozen quote.
“Preparing a batch of English product cards can leave a merchant paying for output they cannot use. SafeRouter sets a fifty-cent ceiling for ten cards. Each card that passes the agreed checks costs five cents.”

00:22–00:47 · Buyer approval
On screen: PayPal Sandbox approval, then the app’s authorization readback. Hide the buyer email and any account details.
“The buyer approves the ceiling once in PayPal Sandbox. The seller reads back the authorization before starting the batch.”

00:47–01:19 · Delivery and repair
On screen: show the local model label. Open one repaired row and one zero-charge row. Keep the controlled fault label visible if using it.
“A local model drafts the cards. SafeRouter checks each product’s identity, specification, price, inventory, and source fields. A failed draft gets one repair. This run uses labeled test faults to show a repair and cards that remain unbillable.”

01:19–01:45 · Final capture
On screen: show the actual accepted count, final charge, and PayPal authorization status returned by the app. Use these words only after observing the real Sandbox run.
“SafeRouter totals the accepted cards and asks PayPal to capture just that amount. Here the confirmation shows [observed count] cards for [observed amount] against the fifty-cent ceiling.”

01:45–02:12 · Receipt
On screen: download the receipt and key, open the separate verifier, verify, and change a line price.
“The verifier checks the signature and recomputes the fee from the individual line items. Changing a line price invalidates the receipt.”

02:12–02:30 · Zero-charge batch
On screen: show a second Sandbox order with the void readback and no capture. Use this narration only after observing the real Sandbox run.
“In the separate all-failed run, SafeRouter voided the authorization and confirmed there was no capture. The merchant can inspect the rejection reasons and start over with corrected product data.”
